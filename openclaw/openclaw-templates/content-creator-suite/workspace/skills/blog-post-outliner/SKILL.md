# Blog Post Outliner

Produce outlines that a writer (or you, in the next step) can turn into a strong piece quickly. Base the angle on the owner's audience and pillars in `brand/brand-profile.md` and `brand/content-pillars.md`.

## Inputs
- Topic or target keyword (required).
- Format: blog post (default), newsletter issue, YouTube script, or LinkedIn article.
- Target length: default 1,200–1,800 words for blogs, 600–900 for newsletters, 6–10 minutes for video.
- Any research notes in `research/` for this keyword.

## Procedure
1. **Search intent.** Decide what the reader searching this wants: learn how (informational), compare options (commercial), or buy (transactional). If `web_search` is available, look at the top results and note what they all cover (table stakes) and what they miss (your angle). Cite the URLs you looked at in the outline's notes.
2. **Angle.** One sentence: why this piece is better or different. Use the owner's experience, opinion or data where possible.
3. **Headlines.** Five options, under 60 characters where possible, primary keyword near the front, no clickbait the content can't deliver.
4. **Structure.** H2/H3 outline in logical reading order. Under each heading: 2–4 bullet points of what to say, plus where an example, story, screenshot or stat from the owner is needed, marked `[owner: add ...]`.
5. **Intro draft.** 60–100 words in the owner's voice: hook, the problem, what the reader will get.
6. **SEO block.** Meta title (≤60 chars), meta description (≤155 chars), URL slug, secondary keywords to work in naturally, 2–3 internal link ideas and 1–2 authoritative external sources to cite.
7. **Call to action** that matches the main offer in the brand profile.
8. **Repurposing hooks.** Three social post ideas this piece can feed.

## Output
Save to `drafts/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>-outline.md` and show the owner the headlines, angle and H2 list in chat. Offer to write the full draft next.

## When writing the full draft
- Follow the outline and the brand voice. Short paragraphs, descriptive subheadings, concrete examples.
- Keep every `[owner: ...]` marker where real experience or proof is needed; never invent it.
- Mention the primary keyword in the title, intro, one subheading and the conclusion, and otherwise write naturally.
