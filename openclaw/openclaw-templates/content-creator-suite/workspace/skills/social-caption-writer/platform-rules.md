# Platform rules

Limits change; if the owner quotes a different limit from the app, use theirs.

## Instagram (feed, carousel, Reels)
- Caption limit 2,200 characters; only the first ~125 show before "more", so the hook must land there.
- Carousels: write slide-by-slide text (slide 1 = hook, last slide = CTA), 10–20 words per slide.
- Hashtags: 3–5 specific ones at the end. Avoid huge generic tags.
- CTA: save, share, comment, or "link in bio".

## LinkedIn
- Post limit 3,000 characters; about 210 show before "see more".
- First line is a standalone hook; then short paragraphs or single lines with white space.
- Professional but human; personal stories with a business lesson perform well.
- Links in the post body reduce reach for many accounts; suggest "link in first comment" if a link is needed.
- 0–3 hashtags.

## TikTok
- Caption up to 4,000 characters, but keep it short (1–2 lines plus keywords people search).
- Deliver: hook line spoken in the first 2 seconds, on-screen text, 3–6 beat script, caption.
- Use 2–4 relevant hashtags or searchable keywords.

## YouTube
- Title ≤100 characters (aim for ≤60), keyword first.
- Description: first 2 lines matter most; include keywords, chapters (timestamps) when a script is given, and links.
- Shorts: same as TikTok structure.

## X (Twitter)
- 280 characters for standard accounts (longer for premium; ask if unsure).
- Threads: hook tweet, 4–8 tweets, one idea each, closing tweet with CTA.
- 0–1 hashtag.

## Threads
- 500 characters per post. Conversational. No hashtag culture beyond one topic tag.

## Facebook
- Short posts (under 80 characters) get more engagement; longer stories work in groups.
- Ask a question to drive comments. Links are fine.

## Pinterest
- Pin title up to 100 characters, description up to 500. Keyword-rich and descriptive, like search queries ("easy weeknight vegan dinner ideas").
- Always include alt text.
