# Compliance rules for product copy

These are general cautions, not legal advice. When a product falls into a sensitive category, keep claims factual and tell the user which statements they should verify.

## Always avoid unless the user supplies evidence
- Superlatives presented as fact: "the best", "#1", "the world's strongest".
- Fake urgency or scarcity: "only 3 left" (unless live inventory), "sale ends tonight" (unless true).
- Social proof you weren't given: review counts, star ratings, "loved by thousands", celebrity use.
- Certifications and standards: FDA-approved, CE, UL, BPA-free, organic, vegan, cruelty-free, FSC, OEKO-TEX, dermatologist-tested. Only include if stated in the input.
- Country-of-origin claims ("Made in USA") unless stated; these are regulated in many markets.
- Warranty or guarantee terms that are not in the input.

## Health, supplements, beauty
- No claims to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent disease ("cures acne", "boosts immunity against colds", "reduces anxiety").
- Prefer structure/function or cosmetic language that describes appearance or feel: "helps skin look smoother", "supports a normal sleep routine" (and only if the input supports it).
- Supplements sold in the US typically need the standard FDA disclaimer when making structure/function claims. Flag this to the user rather than adding it silently.
- Don't say "chemical-free", "toxin-free" or "detox".

## Environmental claims (greenwashing rules apply in the US, UK and EU)
- Avoid vague terms: "eco-friendly", "green", "sustainable", "planet-friendly", "natural".
- Use specific, verifiable facts: "made from 70% recycled polyester", "plastic-free packaging".
- "Biodegradable" and "compostable" need specifics (industrial vs home compostable) and usually evidence.
- "Carbon neutral" only if the user confirms an offsetting or certification programme.

## Children's products and toys
- Don't claim safety ("100% safe", "non-toxic") unless certified and stated.
- Include age grading only if supplied; flag if a small-parts choking warning may be required.

## Electronics and batteries
- Battery life, range, speed and capacity figures only from the input, with conditions if given ("up to 10 hours at 50% volume").
- Compatibility: "compatible with", never implying official affiliation or certification (e.g. "Made for iPhone" is a licensed term).

## Food and drink
- Allergen information only from the input; flag that allergen labelling must match the physical label.
- No health benefit claims ("good for your heart") without approved wording.

## Trademarks
- Do not use other brands' names in titles, tags or keywords except to state genuine compatibility.
- Don't describe products as "like [Brand]" or "[Brand] dupe"; marketplaces remove these.
